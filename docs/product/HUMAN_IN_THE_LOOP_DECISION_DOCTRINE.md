# HUMAN IN THE LOOP DECISION DOCTRINE

## Purpose

TIBER-Fantasy exists to help fantasy football managers make better decisions, not to replace them.

- We help users understand evidence, uncertainty, and tradeoffs.
- We do not promise guaranteed wins.
- We do not aim to remove the manager from the game.

## Core Principle

**TIBER prepares the decision. The human owns the final click.**

## What TIBER Should Do

- Surface relevant evidence from canonical and promoted sources.
- Explain what key signals mean in decision context.
- Show confidence, uncertainty, and readiness state clearly.
- Compare plausible decision paths and outcomes.
- Teach transferable frameworks users can apply beyond one screen.
- Preserve user agency at every action boundary.

## What TIBER Should Not Do

- Silently execute roster, trade, or waiver actions.
- Hide reasoning behind black-box verdicts.
- Present missing, stale, or unavailable data as certainty.
- Turn adapters or UI layers into hidden model brains.
- Claim guaranteed winning outcomes.

## Decision Ladder

1. Observe read-only data and promoted artifacts.
2. Interpret signals and context.
3. Recommend options.
4. Simulate scenarios and tradeoffs.
5. Prepare decision-ready alternatives.
6. **Human decides.**

## Product Rule

**Automate the grind, not the glory.**

## Interpretability Edge

TIBER's edge is not only access to advanced raw metrics.

TIBER's edge is making data and promoted model outputs legible, inspectable, interactive, and decision-useful for real managers making real calls.

## Alignment with Current Architecture Direction

This doctrine aligns with TIBER-Fantasy as a product shell and orchestration layer that:

- Consumes promoted/read-only upstream artifacts.
- Preserves provenance and readiness visibility.
- Avoids local recomputation of upstream model logic.
- Emphasizes explainable decision support over autopilot behavior.
